# Strength Training Just Overtook Weight Loss as America's #1 Fitness Goal — Here's What the 2026 Data Means for You

For the first time in years, "get stronger" has beaten "lose weight" as the nation's top fitness priority — and it's not a vibe, it's a number. In the newly released 2026 Life Time Wellness Survey, **42.3%** of respondents named getting physically stronger as their primary health goal for the year, while **46.5%** said they specifically plan to lift more weights — both edging out weight loss as the stated priority. Eighty-two percent said they're focusing more on overall wellbeing than they did the year before, a 7-point jump.

## It's not a one-survey blip

The same shift shows up in the American College of Sports Medicine's Worldwide Fitness Trends survey for 2026. ACSM renamed one of its long-standing trend categories from "Exercise for Weight Loss" to **"Exercise for Weight Management"** — a deliberate change to reflect that people now train for loss, maintenance, *and* gain, not just the scale. "Balance, Flow and Core Strength" also cracked the top five trends. Longevity was cited by 33.2% of respondents as a leading motivator, running almost neck-and-neck with general fitness at 46.4%. Two independent surveys, same direction: strength and long-term capability are pulling ahead of pure weight loss as why people show up to train.

## Why the shift is happening now

A big part of the story is GLP-1 medications. As more people use them for weight loss, the industry conversation has pivoted hard toward **muscle preservation** — because rapid fat loss without resistance training burns through lean muscle mass along with fat, leaving people lighter but weaker. Trainers and coaches are increasingly positioned around metabolic and strength support rather than calorie-burn-centric programming. Even for people not using these medications, the message has spread: the scale was never the real target — strength, muscle, and the ability to keep doing what you love as you age are.

## What this means for how you train

If your goal-setting has been anchored to a number on the scale, 2026 is a good year to swap it out.

**For general clients:** make resistance training the anchor of your week, not the thing you add if there's time after cardio. Track a different number — the weight on the bar, your rep count, how a lift feels — rather than only the scale.

**For athletes and cricketers:** this national shift is really just the rest of the country catching up to what structured strength & conditioning has prioritized all along. Bat speed, bowling pace, and injury resilience have always been built in the weight room, not just on the pitch.

## Three practical takeaways

1. **Put strength first, not last.** Two to three resistance sessions a week, done before you're fatigued from cardio, beats squeezing in a few sets at the end of a run.

2. **Track load and reps over months**, not the scale week to week. Strength is a lagging indicator you can actually control; body weight fluctuates for a dozen reasons that have nothing to do with your training.

3. **If you're on a GLP-1 medication or considering one,** talk to your coach or doctor about adding resistance training — it's now considered essential to protect the muscle mass you don't want to lose along with the fat.

## The bottom line

The country's fitness goals just realigned with what strength coaches have argued for years: getting stronger is the more durable goal, and everything else — weight management, longevity, even mental health — tends to follow from it.
